How the connections work

PANEL TO PANEL

Locate. Bolt. Tighten.

The blue panel’s pin locates in the orange socket. An M3 screw and washer pull the 4 mm flanges together through a captive metal nut. A 0.3 mm gap allows fitting.

Simplified joint sample using the production interface dimensions; shell contours omitted.
SHELL TO CAR

Clamp the existing post.

Two M3 screws close the rigid collar halves around the unchanged 6 mm post. A separate vertical screw attaches the orange shell seat. Remove that screw to lift off the shell.

Actual clamp halves; seat and post isolated for clarity. Metal hardware has simplified threads.

Put it together

  1. Print the post and joint fit samples; check against the actual posts and nuts.
  2. Print, deburr and inspect the modules and clamp halves.
  3. Insert the captive nuts, locate the panels, then fit the seam screws with underside access.
  4. Fit the collars at the modeled height and tighten their two screws evenly.
  5. Fit the vertical service screws. Check steering, suspension and tool access before driving.

Each print part is one valid connected solid, with all dimensions below 230 mm. The complete STEP preserves 127 original components; only the unused ProSight antenna is omitted. The envelope check does not validate supports, brims, plate layout or minimum wall thickness. Start with the PLA fit samples; verify clamp grip, tightening, wheel travel and low-speed running. M3 is a reasonable prototype size, but the PLA joints and clamp friction set the practical limit. No crash load or tightening torque is rated.

Where the project stands

All four redesigned STEP assemblies passed solid-validity and static interference checks. All 127 retained mechanical components have identical bounds and volumes. The unused external FPV antenna is omitted; the supplied original STEP remains unchanged. The modular revisions add M3 joints and post clamps. Physical fit, steering sweep, suspension travel, and print tolerances still need validation.
